## 2. What changed (inside this run's data window)

Because there is no prior report, this section summarizes the material moves the Fetchers captured within their own lookback windows (roughly 2026-08-25 to 2026-09-01), which double as the baseline going forward.

- **Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks (2026-08-28)** are the single clearest catalyst of the window. On the same day: the dollar index (DXY) jumped from 99.05 to 99.65 (+0.5%), gold began a slide from the ~$4,589–4,656 area down to $4,425–4,456 by 2026-08-31/09-01 (explicitly attributed by the same-day coverage to "hawkish remarks from Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh reviv[ing] expectations for an imminent interest rate hike"), and CME FedWatch's implied odds of a September hike rose from 57% (as read right after the speech) to 66% (by 08-29/31). BTC itself hit a three-month intraday high of ~$81,455 that same day (08-28) before reversing sharply below $78,000 by 08-29. Given the dollar, gold, and rate-odds moves all point to the same Fed-repricing catalyst, this is not a single-cause read forced onto BTC — it is one shared driver visible across four independent series in the same 24–48 hour window, which is why it is treated as a real link rather than a coincidence.
- **BTC price**: from ~$78,745 (08-26) to a $81,455 intraday high (08-28) to a sharp reversal below $78,000 (08-29), settling back into a $77,200–79,200 range by 09-01 (last read ~$78,770). Net: roughly flat to the week's start, but with a sharp round trip through the high.
- **Fed funds path**: a genuine, unresolved conflict this run — CME FedWatch (as reported by secondary sources, since cmegroup.com was blocked) moved from ~58.6% hold (08-25) toward 66% hike-priced (08-29/31), while Polymarket's own "Fed Decision in September?" contract prices 66% odds of no change as of 09-01. These cannot both be describing the same consensus; recorded as an open conflict below, not resolved to one number.
- **Labor market cooling continued**: July payrolls fell -23,000 (an outright decline) after June's already-soft +57,000, with two months of downward revisions (-74,000 combined for April/May). This is the clearest piece of soft-landing-worried data in the set.
- **Stablecoin supply** (a newly added stream this run) fell about $14.4B (4.5%) from its May-2026 peak of $322.4B to $308.0B by mid-August — the sharpest contraction since the Terra collapse — attributed to new federal rules eliminating stablecoin yield, pushing capital into tokenized T-bill/money-market products.
- **Futures leverage**: BTC futures open interest reportedly went from ~$24B (mid-August) to $54.8B (09-01) — more than doubling in about two weeks. This sits awkwardly next to the on-file catalog note that leverage is "meaningfully de-risked vs mid-August" — flagged below as likely stale guidance needing a refresh.
- **New stream**: `stablecoin-supply` entered the catalog this run (see Curator update) — a materiality trigger per the pipeline's threshold rules.
- **Geopolitical**: no single flashpoint newly escalated to a Tier-0 shock this run, but the Russia-NATO incident pattern intensified on multiple fronts (record 2026 airspace incursions over Romania already exceeding all of 2022–2025 combined, NATO fighter scrambles up over 250% year-on-year, NATO shifting shoot-down authority into its own military chain of command) — this pattern is the empirical basis for the `escalation` alternate future below.

**6-month horizon (through ~March 2027)**

| Scenario | Likelihood | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Choppy consolidation with a downside test | ~40% | The Fed picture is genuinely split this run: soft labor data (a payrolls decline in July, two months of downward revisions) and a cooling headline CPI (3.4% y/y in July, down from a 4.2% May peak) argue for easing, while CME FedWatch has swung toward pricing a September hike and Warsh's Jackson Hole tone was explicitly hawkish on the 2% inflation target. With real yields ticking up slightly (roughly 2.31% in July to 2.34–2.41% in August) and the dollar firming on the same hawkish repricing, the base case is a wide, choppy range (roughly $65,000–$85,000) rather than a clean trend, with a real chance of testing the widely-discussed September–November cyclical-low window that several independent analysts have flagged (treated here as one data point, not a forecast to adopt). |
| Dovish surprise / relief | ~30% | If the labor-market weakness and cooling CPI/PCE data dominate the Fed's September decision over the hawkish rhetoric, a hold-with-dovish-tilt or outright pause on hikes would pull real yields and the dollar back down, letting August's strong ETF-inflow pace ($3.03B for the month) resume and pushing BTC back above the $81,455 August high toward new local highs. |
| Hawkish confirmation / risk-off | ~30% | If the Fed follows through on the CME-implied hike path, and this coincides with a fresh shock on any of the live geopolitical or trade fronts (Iran-Hormuz, Russia-NATO incidents, the September 8 Canada retaliation or September 29 pharma-tariff deadlines), credit spreads (currently tight but with a data gap since 08-24) could widen and a stretched equity market (Shiller CAPE near 41.5, historically elevated) could drag BTC down through the equity-correlation channel — a channel that is currently weak (BTC-Nasdaq correlation at a one-year low of 0.38) but, per the stream's own caveat, tends to reassert itself in real stress events. ## 4. Outlook — Alternate future: `escalation` (NATO–Russia escalation)

**Assumptions (from `alternate-futures.md`), and which facts they supersede:**
- A confirmed, Article-5-ambiguous incident against a NATO member occurs within the next six months. This supersedes the `russia-nato` stream's current "elevated but sub-Article-5 background probability" reading with an assumed realized incident.
- Markets react risk-off fast, as with any Tier-0 shock (a shock large and sudden enough to move nearly every asset class at once). This supersedes the current calm-market readings on `dxy` (assume a fast safe-haven dollar spike), `gold` (assume a fast safe-haven rally, on top of its already-elevated 0.52 correlation with BTC), `credit-spreads` (assume fast widening), `equity-valuation`'s transmission channel (assume the already-stretched CAPE market sells off), `btc-nasdaq-corr` (assume correlation rises toward 1, per that stream's own explicit caveat that calm-week readings are weak evidence for a real stress event), `fear-greed` (assume a fast move to extreme fear), and the leverage amplifiers `funding-rates`/`futures-oi-liquidations`/`technical-trend` (assume they amplify the down move, not the direction).

### 4.1 Scenario outlook under `escalation`

**6-month horizon**

| Scenario | Likelihood | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp shock, partial recovery | ~50% | An ambiguous incident triggers an immediate, correlated risk-off move across assets — a plausible 15–30% BTC drawdown from pre-incident levels within days to weeks, alongside a dollar and gold spike and widening credit spreads. Absent an actual Article-5 invocation or open NATO-Russia war, markets typically claw back part of the loss within the six-month window as safe-haven flows fade and rate-cut expectations (a crisis often accelerates central-bank easing) offset the initial shock. Net: still below the pre-incident base case, but off the panic low. |
| Sustained risk-off | ~30% | The incident stays unresolved — prolonged NATO deliberation, tit-for-tat measures, and energy-market disruption (compounding the already six-month-long Iran-Hormuz disruption) keep BTC and risk assets pinned well below pre-shock levels for the full window, potentially testing the $50,000–$65,000 cyclical-low chatter zone harder than the base case would. |
| Fast containment | ~20% | NATO and Russia both step back quickly (ambiguous attribution resolved as accidental or non-state), and markets round-trip back close to the base-case trajectory within the six-month window, leaving only a residual volatility premium. |

**12-month horizon**

| Scenario | Likelihood | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Scar fades, converges near base case | ~40% | The acute crisis phase passes; elevated defense and energy spending create some fiscal stimulus and inflation, but BTC broadly recovers toward its base-case 12-month trajectory, ending only modestly below where the pure base case would have landed. |
| Durable regime shift, pressured | ~35% | The incident marks a lasting change — sustained higher defense spending, a persistent energy-security risk premium, and tighter-for-longer monetary policy to fight the resulting inflation keep BTC and risk assets structurally pressured through the year. |
| Crisis-driven bullish overshoot | ~25% | Full de-escalation plus the monetary and fiscal expansion typically used to offset a growth shock (more M2 growth, easier policy) proves net bullish once the acute shock passes, leaving BTC modestly above the pure base case by month 12. |

**36-month horizon**

| Scenario | Likelihood | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Converges to base case | ~55% | Unless the incident escalates into a sustained NATO-Russia war (a further, lower-probability tail beyond what this future's assumptions stipulate — they specify one incident, not open war), the long-run structural drivers (the 2028 halving, adoption, M2 growth) reassert themselves and the 36-month picture lands close to the base case, just with wider tails on both sides from the residual shock uncertainty. This horizon is the one where the future's assumptions matter least — say so plainly rather than forcing a large structural change. |
| Persistently wider range | ~25% | If defense and security spending structurally elevates inflation and rates for years, the 36-month range is choppier and wider than the base case without a clean breakout. |
| Durable stress | ~20% | If the incident proves to be the opening chapter of a sustained NATO-Russia confrontation, risk premia stay elevated for the full window and BTC underperforms the base case throughout. |

### 4.2 Standing forecast under `escalation` (hypothetical — never tracked or scored)

If the incident occurs, expect an initial sharp drawdown testing the $45,000–$60,000 zone within weeks of the event, consistent with a Tier-0 shock repricing, before the 6-to-12-month recovery-versus-durable-stress dynamics above play out. This is a hypothetical variant only; it does not replace or move the base-case standing forecast in Section 3.2, and it is not scored against actual price.
