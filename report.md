## 3. What changed (since the 2026-09-03 report)

- **BTC pulled back from its early-September highs.** From the Sep-1 close ($77,305.10), BTC rallied to a Sep-2 intraday high of $82,288.10 (briefly clearing the Aug 27/28 highs cited in the last report as "not yet broken"), closed Sep-2 at $81,276.10, then retreated to a Sep-3 close of $79,676.40 and is trading ~$79,660–$79,780 as of this run (three direct API reads — Kraken, Coinbase, CoinGecko — agree within 0.05%). Net: the Sep-2 breakout attempt failed to hold; BTC is down roughly 3% from that intraday peak but still up about 3% from the Sep-1 close. This move is confounded, not attributable to one cause — the same window saw an Iran-driven gold spike, a sharp dollar reversal, and a blowout jobs report, all discussed below.
- **The August jobs report reversed the "softening labor market" narrative.** Released Sep 4: nonfarm payrolls +162,000 versus a ~53,000 consensus (a large beat), unemployment steady at 4.1%. BLS's own revisions also flipped July from the -23,000 decline several recent reports leaned on into a +21,000 gain (revised up 44,000), and adjusted June to a +31,000 baseline (itself in tension with an on-file +57,000 June figure — an unreconciled discrepancy the Fetcher flagged, not resolved here). Scoped strictly to the Fed channel this stream tracks, this flips `labor-market`'s lean from bullish to bearish: strong jobs reduce the case for a near-term cut. This is the most consequential single fact this run — see the materiality flag and Section 5.1.
- **Fed-funds-path re-priced, but two same-day readings disagree on by how much.** A direct Polymarket fetch (2026-09-05) shows 50% hike / 49% hold — a sharp move from Sep 3's clean 42%/59% hold-lean. A separate, secondary "post-jobs" figure (defirate.com, same day) shows only 42.1% hike / 56.9% hold — barely moved. Both are dated after the jobs report; the Fetcher did not reconcile them. Economically, a beat this size should reduce cut odds, which is more consistent with the direct Polymarket reading; this report weighs the scenario shift on that basis while flagging the secondary figure as a live, unresolved disagreement to watch next run. CME FedWatch remains unreachable directly (HTTP 503, a second consecutive run) and its carried-forward figures predate the jobs report.
- **The dollar partly reversed back up, and gold gave back part of its spike — both on the same jobs-report day.** DXY: 98.91 (Sep 3, a sharp one-day drop) → 99.18 (Sep 4, +0.27%), attributed by the Fetcher to the strong jobs data. Gold: $4,480.90 (Sep 3, Iran-driven spike) → $4,420–4,429 (Sep 4, -1.28%), same-day attributed to the jobs beat lifting yields and the dollar. This is a clean, cross-confirmed chain: strong jobs → firmer dollar → weaker gold — not a coincidence across two independently-sourced streams.
