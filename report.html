2. What changed (since the 2026-09-01 report)

  • BTC price roughly flat. Latest confirmed print $77,648.17 (CoinDesk, 09-01) / $77,157.03 (CoinGecko, same day) — essentially unchanged from the prior report's own 09-01 read (~$78,770; the small gap is a same-day source-snapshot difference, not a real move), still inside the $77,200–$79,200 range flagged last run. No 2026-09-02 print was located yet.
  • The hawkish-repricing move has now held for six straight sessions, not just one day. DXY kept climbing through today (2026-09-02): from the 08-21 three-month low of 98.8, through 99.65 on the Jackson Hole day (08-28), to ~99.68–99.71 intraday today. Gold kept falling in the same window, to $4,374.54 today (-1.60% on the day), explicitly attributed by same-day coverage to "surging Treasury yields and rising bets on a September Federal Reserve rate hike" — a two-week low, down from last week's three-month high. Real yields also ticked up over the same window (2.31% in July to ~2.34–2.42% in August across secondary sources), and CME FedWatch's hike odds stayed elevated (57–66% since 08-28/31). Four independent series (dollar, gold, real yields, Fed-odds) are still moving the same direction together, which is why this remains a shared-catalyst read rather than a single-cause claim — but the fact that it has now persisted almost a week, rather than reverting the day after Jackson Hole, is new information; it is the basis for a modest weight nudge in the 6-month table below.
  • BTC–gold correlation climbed further. The 90-day reading is now described as having "topped 50%"/an "all-time high" as of 09-01 (versus 0.52 on 08-26, already a 5-year high), and a 30-day reading separately reached 0.8. The conditional trigger for the gold stream (BTC beginning to track gold) has fired more strongly — but since gold is falling on the same hawkish-repricing story, this link is transmitting bearish, not bullish, pressure for now.
  • Core PCE conflict resolved. Four independent secondary sources (CNBC, Advisor Perspectives/dshort, Fox Business, qz.com) now agree June 2026 core PCE was 3.3% y/y; last run's 2.8% figure is judged an outlier from that run's sourcing, not a genuine reading. This does not change the lean (already bullish via the Fed-easing channel on cooling direction) — it removes an open conflict rather than flipping a call.
  • ISM Manufacturing PMI for August landed, and it decelerated. 54.6% (8th straight month of expansion) but down 1.0 point from July's 55.6% and below the 55.2% consensus; New Orders (-3.0pp), Employment (-1.6pp), and Backlog (-3.2pp) sub-indices all cooled. This breaks the "and rising" half of the stream's own bullish criterion, so the lean softens from clearly bullish (last run, on July's acceleration) to neutral-leaning-mildly-bullish this run. Services PMI for August is due tomorrow (09-03), not yet available.
  • Stablecoin-supply contraction has roughly doubled versus the figure on file. A fresh dated point (09-01) shows aggregate supply at $289.8–290.7B, down about 10% (~$32–33B) from the $322.4B May peak — versus the ~4.5%/$14.6B decline logged just one day ago (08-13 dated point, $308.0B). This is a new, later data point showing the decline accelerating, not a restatement of the same number. It sharpens last run's caution: less of this now looks like a clean rotation into yield-bearing tokenized T-bill products and more like an accelerating net drawdown of ready, on-ramp liquidity — though it remains an amplifier stream (magnitude, not an independent directional cause) per its own polarity.
  • Corporate treasuries reversed direction. Strategy (the largest corporate BTC holder) purchased 4,603 BTC for $369.7M during 08-24–08-30 (avg. $80,318/BTC), ending a 10-week pause and reversing the two sales logged in the prior report; total holdings now 845,050 BTC, funded via an equity raise. The corporate-treasuries lean flips from mildly bearish (last run) to mildly bullish this run — a real direction change, though as an amplifier stream it should be read for magnitude alongside price, not as an independent catalyst.
  • Options vol/skew data refreshed (last run's reading was 55 days stale). DVOL spiked from ~35% to a peak of ~65% intraday on 08-29 (the Jackson Hole/large-options-expiry day, the sharpest DVOL move since early 2023), then cooled to ~43% by 09-01/02; skew flipped from put-heavy to call-heavy during the preceding rally from ~$62,000 to ~$80,000. Confirms the post-Jackson-Hole move carried real amplified magnitude, now partially cooling.

  • Leverage confirmed rebuilding, not "de-risked." The 08-29 sell-off triggered $487.68M in market-wide liquidations (over $360M of it long positions), consistent with leverage amplifying the down move.
